# Terumo BCT Africa Hackathon 2026: Starter Dataset

**One year of a blood system, from donor to patient.** 1 November 2025 to 31 October 2026.

Every team gets the same data so you can spend your 24 hours building, not hunting for data, and so the judges can compare solutions fairly. You are free to use other data too.

## Important: this data is synthetic

Every donor, unit, request and reading in this dataset was generated by a computer model. **No real person, patient or facility record is included.** Facility names are generic and the numbers do not describe the performance of any real hospital or blood centre.

The model is built to *behave* like a real African blood system: it uses Kenyan geography, published reference figures (see Sources) and the problems blood services describe every day. Treat it as a realistic sandbox, not as evidence about any real place.

## The year at a glance

- **45,699** donation attempts in the period; 11% deferred (mostly low haemoglobin), 7.9% of collected donations reactive for an infection and discarded.
- About **6 in 10 donors are students**. When schools close (November to early January, April, August), collections fall and shortages follow.
- **17,430** hospital requests for **34,242** units. 87% fully met. O negative, B negative and A negative patients are least likely to get blood.
- When a hospital runs out, an emergency resupply takes a median of about **5 hours**, much longer for remote counties.
- **About 23%** of red cell and whole blood units are wasted: expired on the shelf, or discarded after the fridge got too warm. At the same time, other facilities run short.
- **2,119 units** were given to patients even though their fridge or cool box had been out of range. Nobody flagged it.

These are the kinds of problems your solution can go after.

## How the system works (in the model)

1. People donate at **drives** run by a regional or satellite centre.
2. Donations are **tested** (1 to 6 days, longer from remote sites). Reactive donations are discarded.
3. Cleared donations are processed at the regional centre into **components**. Centres that cannot separate components send out **whole blood**.
4. Centres **deliver routinely** to hospitals 1 to 3 times a week, based on each hospital's usual order (some over-order, some under-order).
5. Hospitals use stock for **requests**. If they run out, they call the centre for an **emergency** delivery, then other centres.
6. Units **expire** (red cells 42 days, whole blood 35, platelets 5, plasma and cryo 1 year). **Power cuts** without backup push fridges above 6°C; most exposed units are discarded, some are not.

## Deliberate data quality problems

Real systems are messy, so this one is too. These are on purpose:

- About **450 donors (1%) are registered twice** under different IDs (same birth date, sex, county and phone last 4 digits).
- About **1.2% of donor records have no blood group**.
- **14% of requests** have the facility name typed differently (upper case, abbreviations, extra spaces). Use `facility_id` as the truth.
- **5% of emergency requests** record the patient blood group as `unknown`.
- About **3% of transfers** have no `received_at` time (the receiving hospital never confirmed).
- **Temperature loggers drop out** for hours at a time, more often where connectivity is 2G or 3G.
- **60 temperature readings are sensor glitches** (-99.0, 99.9 or 0.0).
- Some donors gave blood again **before the minimum gap** (3 months for men, 4 for women).

## Sources used to set the model's parameters

- Infection rates among donors (HBV 3.5%, HCV 3.2%, syphilis 1.6%, HIV 1.2%): [Seroprevalence and determinants of transfusion transmissible infections among voluntary blood donors in Homabay, Kisumu and Siaya counties](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5848540)
- National collection gap (about 93,000 units against 500,000 needed), staffing and cold chain gaps: [Daily Nation, report on blood transfusion centres](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/report-reveals-shocking-details-of-blood-transfusion-centres-3449136)
- Blood group mix (O+ most common, Rh negative around 1 in 20): approximation from Kenyan donor studies and [KNBTS comments in The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/health/health-science/article/2001438104/check-spouses-blood-group-before-marriage)
- Shelf lives follow standard practice: red cells in additive solution 42 days, CPDA-1 whole blood 35 days, platelets 5 days, frozen plasma and cryo 1 year.
- Seasonality (school terms, December and Easter road traffic peaks, malaria seasons in the Lake and Coast regions) follows the Kenyan calendar.
